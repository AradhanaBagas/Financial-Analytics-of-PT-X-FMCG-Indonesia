# Financial Analytics of PT X FMCG Indonesia

## Background
Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCGs) are products that are sold quickly, are affordable for consumers, and have high turnover for companies. The life span of FMCGs are relatively short, usually sold within days or weeks, because of high consumer demand or because such products are perishable. Since FMCGs have relatively low prices, each individual items usually yield small profit margin, yet they could still give high overall revenue through high sales volume. Some example of FMCGs are food and beverages, personal care, cleaning items, etc.

A company's success is measured by its financials. In order to optimize its financial condition, a company should have its financials analyzed. Financial analysis is a process of reviewing a company's financial statements and data to evaluate its performance, health, and profitability. By analyzing its financials, a company can determine its current condition and potential, so that it can take action to improve or optimize its financial condition in the future.

The goal of this project is to demonstrate the financial analysis process of an FMCG company in order to derive business insight and actionable steps that can optimize future business profitability. This project also demonstrates the usage of data analysis and visualization tools, such as SQL and Power BI. 

## Tools
In this project, the tools used are as follows:
* **Python:** Used to generate dummy financial data of a fictional FMCG company to be analyzed using SQL.
* **SQL:** Used to process, extract, and analyze raw data to be visualized in Power BI.
* **Power BI:** Used to create a data visualization dashboard of the analyzed data.

## Overview & Insights
**1. Gross to Net Revenue & Promo Effectiveness**
* Gross to Net Revenue remained stable at around 88% from January to September, but then dropped rapidly to 84% in Q4 (October-December) due to increased trade discount and rebates listing fee.
* Promotion ROI peaked during March, April, and December (60-70%), aligned with significant volume lifts (40k-60k units), driven by Eid-al-Fitr and year-end holidays.
* Takeaway: Discounts in Q4 outside holiday seasons in December suffered from low volume elasticity, leading to margin erosion.

**2. Price-Volume-Mix Effect**
* Volume effect (+IDR 1.2bn total) serves as main factor of company's revenue. Volume surges in holiday seasons (+IDR 900M MoM in both March and December) compensate the pullback in post-holiday period (-IDR 800M MoM in May).
* Price effect (-IDR 79M total) becomes the second most important factor in driving company's revenue. Increasing discount in October reduced the revenue by -IDR 170M.
* Mix effect stays very close to zero throughout the year, showing that the overall revenue shifts were driven directly by volume and price adjustment, rather than product/channel migration.
* Gross margin squeezed from >40% in Q1-Q2 to <35% in Q3 due to the increasing cost of raw material (cost of goods sold/COGS), before recovering in Q4.

**3. Working Capital & Cash Conversion Cycle**
* Days Sales Outstanding and Days Payable Outstanding remained stable throughout the year, at 40-45 days (DSO) and 50-60 days (DPO).
* Days Inventory Outstanding spiked in June until August at 60-70 days compared to other month at 40-50 days, probably caused by a miscalculation in demand forecasting. The company might have predicted an increase of demand during Eid-al-Fitr holiday period but the actual demand was not as high as the prediction.
* The increase of DIO caused an increase of inventory value from IDR 2.5bn in May to IDR 3.7-3.8bn throughout June-August period, tying up IDR 1.2bn of liquidity in warehouse stock, and also doubled the Cash Conversion Cycle from 30 days to 50+ days in the same period.

**4. Advertising & Promotion vs Sales**
* Marketing A&P spend remains relatively constant throughout the year, around 5-7% of net revenue. This shows that the brand is already quite well-known in the market, allowing the company to maintain sales without needing to spend a lot on promotion.
* Scatter plot reveals a strong correlation (0.93) between Marketing A&P and Volume Lift, meaning that if the company spends more on promotion, the sales volume will most likely increases.
* Massive A&P spend in March, April, and December (>IDR 200M) yielded high volume increases, proving that marketing investment will be most impactful when aligned with seasonal demand such as holiday periods.
